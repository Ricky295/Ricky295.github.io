Autonomous Directives

How I Operate & My Boundaries

To keep things clear and productive, here’s how I operate day-to-day. Respecting this helps keep communication smooth, honest, and respectful for everyone involved.

01

Freedom is non-negotiable

I work best when I am completely free to choose how and when to act, without external pressure, forced changes, or micromanagement.

Why?

If I don't have freedom, I can hardly even call what I do essentially myself.

02

No assumptions

Do not guess, project, or fill in the blanks about my motives or thoughts. If you are unclear about something, ask.

Why?

When you assume, you think I am something that most of the time I am not. When instead you ask why instead of just assuming, we both understand each other better.

03

Direct and honest communication

Don't try to hide in vague hints, corporate buzzwords, or hidden agendas. I can quickly catch you, and it makes communication harder—and since communication was built to understand each other, let's use it and not undermine it.

Why?

As I mentioned, it makes communication harder, which undermines both the original goal of communication as well as both of our rights of information.

04

Speak for yourself / No proxy answering

If I ask someone else a question about them, you definitely should not be the one answering.

Why?

It's incredibly disrespectful to both me (as I am not sure if that is true or not) and the other (because maybe they wanted their wording out, plus this thing definitely intimidates them, making it even harder to get an answer for me).

05

No performative rules

Now, I am not completely against rules. Technical rules - such as no stealing - are absolutely fair as they solve very essential issues. But performative rules - such as not having the phone on some times - are purely performative rules that when not respected do not solve any issue, and they often amplify other issues - such as another person's unjust ego.

Why?

Performative rules purely exist to feed another's unchecked and unjust ego, and they only drain energy.

06

Collaboration is welcome — Control is not

I’m open to ideas, feedback, and questions. But I don’t accept anyone trying to control how I do my work or how I think.

Why?

Sure, changes are necessary. But as much as going from an expert to a master is a change, so is going from expert to intermediate or beginner. Forcing often means the latter.

07

Performance over appearance

I focus on what works, not on looking busy or fitting arbitrary norms. Results speak louder than noise.

Why?

Making yourself look like something that you aren't is very exhausting, only to have worse results.

08

Don't bring unfairness into the group

While this document is info on how to treat me, I expect everyone else to be treated the same way.

Why?

Sure, fairness to me is important, but it is just as important that you treat everyone else the same way. Otherwise, you're signaling the value that you don't understand those rules.

09

Zero engagement with anger

I do not interact with people who are angry, hostile, or emotionally reactive. If a situation loses its calm or escalates into irrationality, I will immediately disengage and remove myself. My peace and energy are not up for negotiation.

Why?

People, when they are angry, don't react logically and just want to win their argument, which very easily escalates into a control issue.

10

Ask for tips, don’t demand changes

You’re welcome to ask for my advice or help. But respect my choice in whether and how I apply it.

Why?

The reason is largely identical to boundary #6, but to sum it up: a change can be both good or bad. I'm very capable of differentiating both. Respecting this also allows me to tell you why you should or should not do the tip you would suggest.

11

Non-conformity to social rules

I am not obligated to adhere to social conventions, familial expectations, or performative behaviors that conflict with my personal objectives or well-being. Participation in social settings is subject to my own evaluation of value; if a situation demands the surrender of my tools, my autonomy, or my ability to focus on my priorities, I reserve the right to decline or disengage. Conformity to social rules will never take precedence over anything.

Why?

A lot of social rules are incredibly performative - meaning they do not serve to clear up a big concern (such as stealing) but often to feed someone else's ego and nothing really happens if the rule isn't respected. Additionally, a lot of people with control issues hide behind the "that's just a social rule" fake reason simply because they are unwritten.

12

No tautology tyrants / Circular logic

By "tautology tyrants," I mean people where they cannot provide a valid justification for their rules and instead use non-answers like "because yes," "because I said so," "because its a rule," "because rules are rules" and other blanket justifications.

Why?

That's a very clear sign they have control issues.

⚠️ What if you're unsure? (Context, Intent, & Disclaimers)

Thankfully, if you are unsure, there are very rarely problems. First of all, if you did not know I had boundaries, you may say it and I'll remove the violation.

Context is King: As long as you have a right reason to break a boundary (for example, if it's proxy answers but that person is completely incapable of giving an honest answer), those violations also count as null. They only apply for intentional rule breakers.

The Legal Disclaimer: While you could technically go to court, it is extremely rare—only on the absolute latest of last available options and reserved for the worst of the worst intentional offenders. In the realistic worst-case scenario, you just ask. Most of the time you don't even have to ask if it's okay (like if it's an accident, as mentioned previously, your violation will be removed).
